This is what all the tracked trajectories say about how the structure forms, which regime forms it, what the MLP is for, and what happens when the belief eigenvalue goes negative.

01

The universal staging order

Every converged run builds the circuit in the same sequence: the linear OV/value circuit first, the geometric attention pattern + belief geometry in a single late transition, and the MLP last of all. Loss plateaus ~80× earlier than the circuit does — a Saxe-style staged transition. (Curves below: Mess3 x=.15, +MLP, MSE, rich init 1/64, seed 0 — representative of all settings.)

Sampling the late regime densely was essential — early-stopped runs miss the entire belief-circuit transition. dim_mid/twonn also shows the predicted non-monotonic path: intrinsic dimension overshoots to ~3.8 at step ~775, collapses, then settles at ~1.5 (the 2-D belief simplex).

02

What the MLP is for: the nonlinear part of the readout

Adding the MLP leaves the pre-MLP constrained geometry untouched and improves the post-MLP Bayesian fit — the paper’s mid→constrained / final→Bayesian refinement. Its workload scales with how nonlinear the job is: the constrained→Bayesian gap (slow-mixing > fast-mixing) and the loss readout (CE makes it load-bearing; MSE barely uses it).

MLP share of the output logit · dla/mlp

CE reads beliefs out as log(b·E) — a nonlinearity the linear paths can’t supply, so the MLP carries half the logits. MSE reads them out linearly (b·E), leaving the MLP only the ~3% nonlinear remainder on fast-mixing x=.15.

03

Init scale picks the circuit, not the loss

Feature movement ‖Δθ‖/‖θ₀‖ collapses ~5 orders of magnitude with init — the textbook lazy↔rich signature. Small init = rich = finds the Javan circuit (attention decays at η ≠ eigenvalue); large init = frozen = underfits via a non-geometric circuit. Robust across loss, bias, lr, and a frozen warm-up. (Mess3 x=.15, endpoint.)

Attention decay base · target η*≈0.45 (< ζ=0.55)

The model discounts older tokens faster than the process eigenvalue (η < ζ) — a matched filter, not a readout of the belief operator. Confirmed on all three processes (two-state η=0.41<λ=0.50; x=.15 η*≈0.45<ζ=0.55; x=.05 η*≈0.75<ζ=0.85).

The flat landscape underneath. The attention-controlled (skip-bigram) term is only ~0.19% of the loss, so under freeze-forever the model still reaches near-optimal loss with uniform attention (decay 1.0) — same loss, different circuit. The value circuit even forms (+0.057) with attention pinned. Quality lives in decay→η and value→+0.08, not in the loss.

04

Negative eigenvalue (x=.42, ζ=−0.26): one head suffices

When x>⅓ the belief eigenvalue goes negative, so a token’s contribution alternates sign with lag (ζⁿ). The paper’s reason for needing two heads: a softmax head is a non-negative filter and can’t alternate. Our data refines this — at x=.42 one head reaches optimal loss and the second head is unused, because the alternating part is loss-negligible.

The model fits almost entirely from the current token (direct path ≈ 1.0); the attention/OV history is near-idle and the lag≥2 contribution is ~0 — the sign-alternating tail that would require two heads is simply never used (|ζ|²=0.07 is loss-negligible). Where the OV does act (lag-1), it anti-copies by flipping its sign (ov_cos −1): non-negative attention × negated OV = the negative lag-1 correction. 1-head and 2-head have identical DLA, loss, and spectrum.

Open. The 2-head necessity should bite only closer to x→0.5 (slower oscillatory decay → lag≥2 matters). Not yet tested: x=0.5, length-dependence, per-head attention/OV inspection. This is the lead-in to Milestone 2 / Theme C.
